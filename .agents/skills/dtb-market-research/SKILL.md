# DTB Market Research

## Scope first
Define question, market/geography, product segment, timeframe, comparison basis, and decision the research must support. Separate modes when useful: landscape, competitor deep dive, product/spec benchmark, pricing, demand signal, news pulse, whitespace, supply/regulatory risk, or contractor sentiment.

For competitor-landscape work, define whether the user needs a focused comparison panel or a broad market map. Never describe a result as “all competitors” or “complete” without an explicit, reproducible coverage boundary and audit. For DTB's standing storefront comparison, preserve Al's Taping Tools, All-Wall, and Wall Tools as the three core competitors; add no more than seven additional storefronts, and only when customer, product, channel, or service overlap is evidenced. This ten-storefront ceiling applies to the main comparison panel, not to the separate manufacturer/brand benchmark list. Keep incidental leads out of the panel unless they affect the decision. Separate (1) manufacturers/brand owners, (2) direct-to-consumer brand stores, (3) specialist ecommerce retailers, (4) building-material distributors and local branches, (5) marketplaces/sellers, (6) repair/rental/refurbishment providers, and (7) adjacent/substitute solutions. A company can occupy multiple roles; record evidence for each role. Separate legal/operator entity, brand, storefront/domain, parent company, channel relationship, and geography. Do not infer ownership, authorization, exclusivity, manufacturing, or affiliation from product listings or similar names.

### Focused discovery and verification
1. Freeze the decision scope, geography, customer segment, product families, seller types, and exclusions. For DTB's core panel, prioritize U.S. storefronts where professional drywall contractors can buy automatic/semi-automatic taping and finishing tools, parts, and closely related equipment. Include repair/rental only when it competes with a DTB service or creates a material alternative.
2. Preserve the three core competitors, then use bounded discovery to select up to seven additional storefronts. Rank candidates by demonstrated assortment overlap, buyer overlap, service overlap, U.S. availability, and ability to observe their offer online. Do not add retailers merely to increase the count.
3. Maintain a separate, selective manufacturer/brand benchmark list. Derive brands from the DTB canonical catalog, first-party manufacturer catalogs/support, and products listed by core panel stores. Search only enough to verify brands material to those competitors and DTB's product decisions; do not build a manufacturer census unless requested.
4. Verify each panel member on its own active first-party site or another authoritative source. For a storefront, verify live domain, in-scope category/product, apparent buy/contact/order path, served geography, and whether it is a direct brand store, retailer, distributor, marketplace seller, or lead-generation page. For a manufacturer/brand owner, use first-party about/product/support/catalog material. Mark uncertain role or relationship “unverified”; do not promote a listing to confirmed manufacturer/authorized dealer.
5. Deduplicate aliases/domains only with evidence. Record redirects, acquisitions, discontinued/redirected domains, successor brands, and stale directory listings as dated relationship claims. Keep distinct legal entities and storefronts distinct until a source establishes common ownership.
6. Maintain concise query/source notes, candidate disposition (panel, manufacturer benchmark, watchlist, duplicate, inactive, out of scope, unresolved), and evidence register. For every confirmed panel entry, store canonical name, role, brand assortment, parent/operator if evidenced, domain, relevant product families, geography, first-party source URL(s), retrieval date, status, and confidence.
7. Refresh volatile fields separately. Recheck storefront activity, brand assortment, dealer authorization, branch coverage, repair/rental availability, and prices before relying on them. A directory is not live inventory evidence; a search result/snippet is not page verification. Preserve prior dated observations instead of overwriting history.

For each research run, state tools/sources actually used and their coverage. If a market-intelligence provider returns no entities/data for this specialist niche, report it as a coverage gap, not a null market. Do not claim sales, market share, product demand, authorization, or manufacturer identity from catalog counts, prices, snippets, or scraped retailer metadata alone. Use the retailer price-research pipeline only for its documented competitor/product-price scope; it supports the core three-storefront panel and is not a census of the market. Keep the main comparison panel at ten storefronts maximum unless the user changes that limit.

## Evidence hierarchy
Use manufacturer/provider/regulator sources for authoritative facts; reputable retailer/distributor sources for observed current pricing/availability; credible trade/statistical sources for broader market claims; and community sources for sentiment/anecdote only. Search snippets are discovery aids, not sufficient evidence for consequential claims.

Record retrieval dates for volatile facts. Normalize units, currency, pack size, configuration, tax/shipping assumptions and product generation before comparing numbers. Clearly distinguish MSRP/list, sale price, marketplace price, advertised price and out-of-stock observations.

Corroborate important or contested claims where feasible and note contradictions rather than averaging incompatible evidence. Separate sourced fact, calculation, inference, hypothesis and recommendation. Never fabricate private DTB sales, demand, margin or conversion metrics.

## Output quality
Lead with decision-relevant conclusions, then show evidence and limitations. Include comparable tables/calculations when they improve understanding, source provenance and dates, confidence, missing data, and concrete implications. Do not turn weak proxies (search count, review count, social mentions) into claimed sales volume without a defensible methodology.
