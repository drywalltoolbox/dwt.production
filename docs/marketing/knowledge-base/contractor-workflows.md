# Contractor workflow and drywall tool domain map

**Last curated:** 2026-10-30. Workflow descriptions summarize the 2026-10-20 industry research and manufacturer materials listed in the dated sources; they are a domain map, not universal job instructions.

## Typical finishing workflow map

1. **Scope the job and choose a system:** work volume, surface/layout, crew experience, access, and existing equipment influence the choice among hand tools, semi-automatic tools, automatic taping/finishing systems, and adjacent equipment. Verify each product's manufacturer-stated purpose and configuration.
2. **Prepare tools and compound:** identify the correct tool, setup, compatible components, and compound-handling instructions from its manual. Tool-specific procedures vary.
3. **Apply tape and compound:** tapers, banjos, compound tubes, corner tools, and hand tools serve different tasks. Do not imply automatic tools eliminate all hand work.
4. **Finish flats, corners, and fasteners:** flat boxes, angle heads, corner rollers/finishers, nail spotters, skimming blades, and knives each address distinct finishing surfaces and stages.
5. **Feather, inspect, and correct:** hand finishing and detail work may remain necessary; exact process depends on job, materials, and finish standard.
6. **Clean and maintain:** cleaning, lubrication, inspection, and wear-part replacement are product-specific. Use current manuals and approved parts.
7. **Diagnose, repair, or replace:** identify brand, exact family/model, generation and relevant serial clues; use official schematic/callout and part identifiers; confirm fit before recommending a part or repair route.

## Product-family map

| Family | Examples | Key buying/support questions |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic taping | Tapers, tape cutters, pumps, goosenecks | Model/generation, setup, weight/handling, maintenance, repair path, included components |
| Automatic finishing | Flat boxes, angle heads, corner tools, nail spotters, handles | Working width, head size, reach, exact wear-part fit, system compatibility |
| Semi-automatic | Banjos, compound tubes, applicators, corner rollers/flushers | Intended task, fittings, cleanup, included accessories, compatibility |
| Hand tools | Knives, pans, hawks, trowels, corner knives, skimming blades | Size, material, edge/flex, grip, use case |
| Parts and maintenance | Seals, blades, wheels, cutter assemblies, kits, lubricants | Exact model and generation, part number, kit contents, safe procedure |
| Adjacent equipment | Sanders, dust control, texture sprayers, mixers, stilts | Whether the product overlaps a DTB customer job or is simply adjacent |

## Parts discovery sequence

A strong customer-facing research path is: identify the tool → find model/generation clues → locate the manufacturer schematic/manual → identify callout and part number → confirm exact fit and kit contents → choose part, repair, or replacement route. This is an opportunity hypothesis, not a claim of proven conversion impact. Avoid request-time fuzzy identity resolution or universal compatibility claims.
