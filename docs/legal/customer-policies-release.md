# Customer Privacy Policy and Terms of Service — release contract

**Owner:** Customer-facing frontend presentation in `frontend/src/pages/legal/`. WooCommerce remains the owner of checkout/order/refund operations; DTB backend and providers remain data-processing authorities. These pages are legal disclosures, **not** new backend policies or processing logic.

## Routes and content

- `/privacy-policy/` → `PrivacyPolicy.jsx` — customer data handling for browsing, accounts, payment, checkout, shipping, returns, repairs, support, security, providers, and privacy choices.
- `/terms-of-service/` → `TermsOfService.jsx` — customer purchases, order acceptance, shipping, returns, warranties, service authorizations, permitted site use, and liability.
- Both reuse `LegalDocument.jsx`, the existing SEO/canonical logic, accessible in-page navigation, and responsive design. Store Policies and SEO titles must describe the customer policies, not the Intuit operator integration.
- Accounting/QuickBooks may be mentioned *as an actual or conditional processing category* in the customer Privacy Policy. The former QuickBooks-specific EULA and integration privacy notice should be preserved separately if required for the Intuit app listing or operator governance, rather than published at the consumer route.

## Release verification required

1. **Business identity:** Confirm the legally formed operator, its required registered/mailing address, operating jurisdiction, public privacy email (`dtb@drywalltoolbox.com`), and contact response process. Internal draft `docs/company/dtb-operating-agreement.md` is **not proof of formation**; do not advertise `Drywall Toolbox LLC` solely from that draft. Add identity/address to legal content if legally required.
2. **Data flow/provider inventory:** Compare disclosures to active SiteGround, WooCommerce, checkout gateways, Veeqo, QuickBooks, email delivery/marketing, address autocomplete, Taper/chat assistant, support storage, analytics, tracking pixels, embeds, and any plugins that process visitor or customer data. Identify subprocessors, roles, enabled modules, and cross-border transfers.
3. **Cookie/privacy rights compliance:** Inspect actual production cookies/storage and consent behavior. Confirm opt-in controls for nonessential technologies where required, opt-outs for targeted advertising / sale or sharing where relevant, supported privacy signals, request/appeals handling, and state/EEA/UK-specific requirements if customers are in scope. Do not treat a text disclosure as a technical consent mechanism.
4. **Retention and security:** Approve system-specific schedules (including orders, repairs/photos, support, logs, marketing contacts, backups, third-party ledgers), retention exceptions, access controls, breach reporting, and provider transfer safeguards. The customer copy intentionally avoids inventing fixed durations.
5. **Commercial policy reconciliation:** Verify the published 45-day eligible standard return policy, cancellations, exclusion lists, refunds, warranty terms, repair diagnostic fees/preapproval, handling of unclaimed tools, shipping dates and costs, and any quote-specific agreements. `frontend/src/pages/FAQ.jsx`, `ReturnPolicy.jsx`, `ShippingPolicy.jsx`, and repair routes currently contain additional specific commitments and should remain consistent with these Terms.
6. **Checkout legal continuity:** On October 23, 2026, the live WordPress options read `wp_page_for_privacy_policy=3` and `woocommerce_terms_page_id` empty. WooCommerce's checkout privacy text contains `[privacy_policy]`. Verify the checkout block's rendered Privacy link resolves to the **correct customer policy** on the production origin; set/assign a suitable Terms page and checkout presentation through the WooCommerce owner when needed. React route content alone does not ensure native checkout legal links are correct.
7. **Legal review:** Have qualified counsel assess enforceability, any required company address, required disclosures, jurisdiction-specific rights and remedies, liability terms, electronic assent, and policy effective date before public activation.
8. **Build and UI validation:** Run `cd frontend && npm run test:legal && npm run lint && npm run build:staging` (and production build for production release). Test both direct-navigation URLs and internal links, mobile/desktop table of contents, keyboard/focus navigation, deep-link hashes, screen readers, print layout, SEO/canonical URLs, and external privacy links.
9. **Deployment:** A GitHub commit/PR does **not** deploy to SiteGround. After approved merge, use the existing release assembly/upload workflow, then verify rendered pages and links on staging and production. Confirm the native WooCommerce checkout is still a full-document handoff with intact order/payment authority.

## Non-goals

No change to checkout processing, order creation, payment providers, backend authorizations, WooCommerce retention, privacy consent implementation, or QuickBooks synchronization. No invented provider-activation claims or fabricated business details. Do not replace the authoritative WooCommerce/WordPress checkout policy hooks with client-side legal text.
